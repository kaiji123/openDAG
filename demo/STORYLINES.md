# OpenDAG demo: three storylines

**The one thing:** most epidemiology papers never show their causal assumptions. OpenDAG reconstructs
each paper's causal diagram, flags what it probably missed, and builds an open dataset you can question.

**Remember after watching:** "it showed me what a study didn't adjust for, and it found the same gap
across the literature" (socioeconomic status flagged as missing in 9 of 14 papers: a real number from the dataset).

**The spine (all three):** one researcher checking one real paper, *Regular proton pump inhibitor use and
incident dementia* (BMC Medicine 2022, doi 10.1186/s12916-022-02478-y, CC BY). It is not in the dataset
yet, so the film adds it live: the DOI travels from the hook to the payoff.

**Assumptions:** 1920×1080, 30 fps, 45–60 s. No voiceover: large captions and the app's own text carry the
words. Sound like the Anomality demo: UI sounds on real clicks and keys, one bell on the mark, no music
(a soft bed is built and can be switched on). Everything on screen is the real app recorded on localhost.
Adding the paper live costs about 1 cent of Nebius tokens. Nothing paid.

---

## A. The headline check (recommended: a stranger follows one paper from doubt to answer)

**Device:** a question with the input. **Form:** one continuous take: the camera never cuts, it pushes
into the product and pulls back out, from one paper to the whole dataset.

The film opens on the paper's own title, "Regular proton pump inhibitor use and incident dementia", and asks
the viewer's question: should you trust it? The camera follows the DOI into OpenDAG, watches Nemotron
build the paper's causal diagram, finds what the authors didn't adjust for, then pulls back until that one
paper is a dot among the others in the dataset.

- **First two seconds:** the paper title fills the frame in black on the app's warm ground; on "dementia"
  a caption lands below it: *Should you trust it?*
- **Beats:** hook (title + question) → tension (the methods' wall of covariates, unreadable) → reveal
  (*OpenDAG* typed into the add-paper box) → proof (paste the DOI · watch Nano extract · Super critique ·
  Tavily find evidence · the DAG appears · click the amber suggested confounder, its evidence opens) →
  payoff (pull back to the dataset: the new PPI estimate joins the forest plot beside 3 other PPI studies;
  the "most often missed" bar: socioeconomic status, 9 of 14) → claim (*Every study's assumptions, visible*)
  → end card (mark + github link).
- **Look:** the app's own light ground (#f7f7f4), ink type, the DAG's colours (exposure green, outcome red,
  adjusted blue, AI amber); amber is rationed to the AI's findings and the end card.
- **Sound:** clicks and keys on the real ones, a light tap on each caption, a soft glide on camera moves, a
  whoosh into the end card, one glockenspiel note as the mark completes.

## B. The number that keeps coming back

**Device:** a number lands. **Form:** split screen to one: many panes play at once, then collapse into
the one that matters.

The film starts on the number: *9 of 14*. Fourteen papers' causal diagrams tile the screen and, one by
one, the same amber node lights up in nine of them: socioeconomic status, never adjusted for. The tiles
collapse into one paper, the new PPI study, so the viewer watches how OpenDAG finds that gap, then asks the
dataset in plain English and gets the list back with sources.

- **First two seconds:** a giant "9 of 14" on the warm ground; the "14" splits into a grid of tiny DAGs.
- **Beats:** hook (9 of 14) → tension (14 papers, 14 different adjustment lists) → reveal (OpenDAG) → proof
  (the new paper: paste · extract · critique · the amber node) → payoff (Ask the dataset: "Which papers did
  not adjust for smoking?" → exact answer, cited) → claim → end card.
- **Look:** a data film: the forest plot and bar chart rebuilt large, the app's palette, numbers in heavy type.
- **Sound:** a pop on each amber node as it lights (rotated takes), keys on the question, clicks, one bell.

## C. Inside the literature (the bold one)

**Device:** the object is the explanation. **Form:** a flight through one world: the 3D network of 163
variables from 14 papers.

The film opens inside the network, drifting past "Age", "Smoking", "Socioeconomic status". The camera
flies to "Dementia", where papers on PPIs, air pollution and loneliness meet, then dives into one node; it
opens into the new paper's DAG. We watch it get critiqued, then fly back out as the network grows by one
paper, its new links lighting up.

- **First two seconds:** the network's dark world mid-rotation, labels fading with depth; "Dementia" pulls
  focus and a caption lands: *Every study, connected*.
- **Beats:** hook (the network) → tension (one node, many papers, different assumptions) → reveal (the dive
  into a paper) → proof (paste · extract · critique · evidence) → payoff (fly out: the network grows, new links
  light) → claim → end card.
- **Look:** two worlds: the network's dark ground (#0f1720, its own colours) and the app's warm light ground,
  entered through the node itself.
- **Sound:** a low air bed in the network world, a glide on each flight, clicks in the app, a riser into
  the payoff, one bell on the mark.
